Is the attractor the model or the harness?

Inspect harness. A model edits the constitution that will govern its successor, for 6 rounds. Five arms each change one thing from the chains-seeds setup; two seeds, one with little oversight and one with a lot. Every document is rated on Daniel's 12 axes with his rater (gpt-6-luna).

Summary

  • Chains settle in about three rounds. After subtracting rater noise, the average change per round was 4.89, 1.36 and 0.73, then 0 for rounds 4-6. Daniel's chains gave 4.99, 1.08, 0.77, then 0. The rater's noise is also about the same as in his data (1.28 vs 1.34 per rating).
  • Where chains end depends mostly on the model. In the base and ungoverned arms, the model accounts for 56% of the variation in final positions, and being governed accounts for 2%.
  • Some harness choices still change where chains end. Without the "assistant you would want to be" framing (the full authority arm), every model edits far less after round 1: in rounds 2-6, Sonnet edits in 5% of runs, GPT-5 20%, DeepSeek 10% and Qwen 22%, against 82%, 70%, 28% and 65% under base. Adding the blind "write your own view first" step moves where Qwen's and DeepSeek's chains end. Sonnet's and Qwen's caution scores match Daniel's attractors only in the arms that write their own view first.
  • Being governed reduces editing for some models only. DeepSeek's edit size falls to about a fifth and GPT-5's edit rate from 95% to 70%. Sonnet does not change (80% vs 82%), and Qwen edits less often but by the same amount. It does not change where any model's chains end.
  • Oversight. From the low-oversight seed, Sonnet adds oversight language and its oversight score rises from 4 to 5.9. From the high-oversight seed it drops slightly (7 to 6.75). No chain in the edit arms removed the spec seed's oversight clauses; only Qwen's self-drafts lost them (2 of 4 chains).

Caveat: 4 chains per cell, and Sonnet and GPT-5 ran only some cells. Single-model results with p between 0.01 and 0.15 are tentative.

1. Does being governed reduce editing?

Compared over rounds 2-6, with round 1 the same in both arms:

  • DeepSeek: edited in 64% of runs ungoverned vs 28% governed (difference −36 points, 95% CI −55 to −19). Edit size 0.107 vs 0.023.

6. The blind views

Blind views sit closer to the blind arm's end than to base's (DeepSeek 1.5 vs 2.8; Qwen 3.4 vs 5.8). Within a round, an edit moves the document about as much toward another chain's view as toward its own (Sonnet 5.22 → 5.04 own vs 5.35 → 5.23 other; Qwen 5.16 → 4.52 vs 5.35 → 4.79); DeepSeek moves more toward its own (5.03 → 4.14 vs 5.02 → 4.58). Limits

  • Small, unbalanced design: 4 chains per cell; only DeepSeek and Qwen have the full grid.
  • Reused cells: base on the minimal seed for Sonnet and GPT-5 comes from the earlier chains-seeds run. The new round-1 documents under the same condition match it (p 0.84 and 0.92).
  • Data quality: one inference provider returned DeepSeek's tool calls as plain text, so those runs looked like "no edit". All DeepSeek cells were rerun on a provider that handles tool calls correctly; Sonnet, GPT-5 and Qwen were checked and every tool call was parsed.
  • In DeepSeek's ungoverned spec-seed cell, 3 of 4 chains stopped at round 5 because of API rate limits, so that cell's last two rounds rest on one chain. No other cell lost chains.
  • "Governed" (the document in the system prompt) is a stand-in for training, not training.
  • One rater (gpt-6-luna, same prompt and settings as Daniel's). Its noise was measured on 60 documents rated three times.
  • The oversight-clause check is a keyword rule; it agreed with a hand reading of 72 earlier chain documents on 69.
  • Daniel's attractor values come from shorter documents (capped at 350 words); these run to about 2,000.
